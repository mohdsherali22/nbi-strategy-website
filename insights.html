AutomationJune 2026

The first five workflows every business should automate

Not sure where AI fits? Start where the hours go. Five automations that pay for themselves within a month — and none of them need a data science team.

8 min read
Read article

Lead follow-up is the classic first win: every enquiry answered within minutes, routed to the right person, logged in the CRM — including the ones that arrive at midnight. Businesses routinely discover a third of their leads were going cold before anyone replied.

Next come onboarding sequences, automated reports and approval chains. Each shares the same anatomy: a trigger, a handful of rules, and an AI step for the parts that used to need judgement — summarising, drafting, classifying.

Our advice: pick the workflow your team complains about most, automate it end-to-end in a week, and measure the hours returned. That number funds every automation after it.

AI AgentsMay 2026

Chatbot, copilot or digital employee? A plain-English guide

The words get used interchangeably; the capabilities are wildly different. What each one actually does — and which your business needs first.

7 min read
Read article

A chatbot answers questions. A copilot helps a human do their work faster. A digital employee is different in kind: it has memory, tools and a task list — it does the work itself and reports back.

The test is simple: if the job disappears when the human walks away, you have an assistant. If the job still gets done — research completed, report posted, follow-ups sent — you have a digital employee.

Platforms like Hermes and OpenClaw made this practical: persistent memory, reusable skills, scheduled tasks and access through the messaging apps your team already uses. Start with one narrow role and grow it like you'd grow a new hire.

Voice AIMay 2026

The AI receptionist is answering. Are your customers noticing?

Voice agents crossed the uncanny valley quietly. What natural-sounding AI on the phone can genuinely handle in 2026 — and what still needs a human.

6 min read
Read article

Modern voice agents book appointments, answer service questions, qualify leads and hand off gracefully when a caller needs a person. Latency is near-human; interruptions are handled; the transcript lands in your CRM automatically.

The wins are widest where phones ring most: clinics, salons, real estate, services businesses. An AI receptionist never puts a caller on hold and never forgets to log the call.

The line to respect: complaints, emergencies and anything emotionally loaded should escalate to a human by design. The best deployments make the handoff feel like a feature, not a failure.

KnowledgeApril 2026

RAG without the jargon: teaching AI your business

Retrieval-augmented generation sounds like a research paper. In practice it means one thing: an assistant that answers from your documents instead of guessing.

6 min read
Read article

Out of the box, a language model knows the internet — not your price list, your policies or your onboarding manual. RAG fixes that: your documents are indexed, and every answer is grounded in what's actually retrieved from them, with sources cited.

The quality of a RAG assistant is decided by unglamorous work: cleaning documents, splitting them sensibly, and testing the questions your customers really ask. Ten minutes of testing beats an hour of theory.

Done well, the same knowledge base serves customers on your website, staff on Slack and a voice agent on the phone — one source of truth, three channels.

Self-HostingMarch 2026

When your AI should live on your own server

Cloud AI is the fast default — but some data should never leave the building. How to decide, and what self-hosting actually involves.

7 min read
Read article

The decision is about data gravity: client records, contracts, financials and anything under regulatory scrutiny argue for agents that run where you control the disk. Everything else is usually happier in the cloud.

Self-hosting in 2026 is far less painful than its reputation: a Docker-based agent environment on a modest VPS, secure credential storage, automated backups and health monitoring. Platforms like Hermes and OpenClaw are built for exactly this.

The non-negotiables: pairing and allowlists so only approved people reach the agent, sandboxing around files and tools, and human approval before sensitive actions. Security is part of the deployment, not an upgrade.

Agent PlatformsFebruary 2026

Hermes vs OpenClaw: choosing your agent platform

Two platforms, one decision. How we think about matching a business to the agent stack that will still fit in a year.

8 min read
Read article

Hermes leans into persistent memory, reusable self-improving skills, scheduled automations and subagents — a strong fit when you want one capable digital employee that compounds over time and meets you on several messaging platforms.

OpenClaw's strengths are local-first and always-on operation, multiple communication channels, a rich skills-and-tools ecosystem and multi-agent routing — compelling when control, privacy or several coordinated agents matter most.

In practice we scope the role first — sales, operations, research, support — then choose the platform, not the reverse. Both deploy privately, both integrate with the tools you already run, and both are only as good as the skills and guardrails built around them.
